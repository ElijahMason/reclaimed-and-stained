Wood

The board already served. Put it back to work.

Donated reclaimed wood is the stock. It comes from construction, patios, decking, and similar building. The shop prepares it for the next job.

Pull the nails

The shop pulls the nails and the other fasteners. A board that still holds metal is not ready to read, and it is not ready for a job.

Read the board

A reading is a close look and a note on the piece. The note covers grain, checks, twist, wane, remaining thickness, soft spots, and ends that can still be cut square. When the grain names a species, the note says so. When it does not, the note stays with what the eye can check.

The note records what the piece can still do. The note does not assign a structural grade.

Keep or pass on

The shop keeps a board for a deck, a length to cut for a patio, or a stick for a repair, when that piece can still do a job.

The shop passes on deep rot, a break through the section, or metal that will not come out. Passed-on wood does not go out for building.

The builder confirms the fit

The reading is a record of what the eye found. Your builder confirms a piece fits the job. Load, span, and fasteners stay with the builder.

Stain, when the piece needs it

When a kept piece gets a stain, the shop writes a label from the stain card. The label names a flat color in shop language. A label does not mean every board wears that finish, and it does not change the builder's call.
